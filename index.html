color-semantic spaces alignment

Ciaccio, Gatti, Vecchi & Rinaldi

Here we present the methodological details and the results of the reanalysis of the PNAS study by Mukherjee et al. (2026) and of the BRM study by Keha, Henik and Kalanthroff (2026). We re-analyzed the released colour-association ratings for all 70 concepts from Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS) and the representative RGB colours for all 143 words distributed by Keha et al. (2026, BRM). We compared the colour and semantic spaces using three complementary English fastText models, three-dimensional multidimensional scaling (MDS) and Procrustes analysis.

The same three semantic models and comparison procedure are used in both datasets. Each dataset has its own colour configuration and three semantic configurations. The six tests therefore compare matched sets of concepts within each dataset.

2. Data and concept matching

Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS): 70 concepts represented by colour-association profiles

The colour input is all_ratings_mat.csv from the repository accompanying Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS). It contains mean association ratings for 70 concepts and 71 colour patches. We included the original 30 concepts and the additional 40. A row is a concept; its 71 values describe the strength of association with every patch in the palette.

We used these released mean ratings directly and reconstructed the three-dimensional colour configuration by applying MDS to their cosine dissimilarities.

Keha et al. (2026, BRM): 143 concepts represented by mean RGB colours

This reanalysis uses Experiment 1 of Keha et al. (2026, BRM). Participants identified an associated basic colour, rated the strength of the association and selected a colour with a continuous RGB picker. We included the data for all 143 words distributed by the authors.

For each concept, we took the first-order mean RGB triplet from color_data.xlsx, retaining its full numerical precision. These values agree with the corresponding summary in Full_Item-Level_Data.xlsx and reproduce the published Supplementary A1/A2 RGB values after rounding to integers. The preparation code converts each mean RGB triplet once to CIELAB under the standard sRGB/D65 assumption. CIEDE2000 differences are then calculated between the resulting Lab colours.

This processing starts from the published item summaries. It does not filter individual responses or average Lab values calculated separately for individual trials. The original mean RGB values and the derived Lab values are both retained in the processed workbook.

3. Semantic models

All three models are English fastText spaces with 300 dimensions and a context window of five words. FastText incorporates character n-grams into distributional word representations. The models differ in corpus, training objective and subword settings; each receives the same analysis and has equal standing in the comparison.

cc.en.300English Common Crawl and WikipediaCBOW with position weights5

cc.en.300 and wiki.en are the pretrained models described in the Common Crawl and Wikipedia fastText releases. The colorless_EN model was trained for this project on the corpus used by Baroni et al. (2014), comprising approximately 2.8 billion tokens before colour-term removal. Its training description specifies removal of the 56 terms listed below before fastText training.

The first two models assess correspondence under different standard corpus and training configurations. The colorless model adds a semantic space learned after removal of the specified colour vocabulary. For every model, all 300 features contribute to the semantic dissimilarities from which MDS is calculated.

4. From input features to Procrustes tests

  1. Calculate dissimilarities. For the colour profiles from Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS) and for all semantic vectors, calculate one minus cosine similarity. For the colours from Keha et al. (2026, BRM), convert the mean RGB triplets to Lab using farver::convert_colour(..., from="rgb", to="lab", white_from="D65", white_to="D65"), then use farver::compare_colour(..., from_space="lab", method="cie2000"). This produces one colour distance matrix and three semantic distance matrices per dataset.
  2. Construct three-dimensional configurations. Apply cmdscale(as.dist(d), k=3, eig=TRUE, add=FALSE) separately to each distance matrix. This is classical MDS, retaining coordinates associated with the three largest positive eigenvalues of the double-centred squared-distance matrix. Dissimilarities are not square-root transformed, and no additive correction is applied.
  1. Fit each semantic configuration to the colour configuration. Use vegan::protest(colour, semantic, permutations=permutations). The function centres the coordinate columns, normalises both configurations to unit Frobenius norm and optimises rotation/reflection and overall scaling. The colour configuration is the reference for all three models within a dataset.
  2. Evaluate alignment against shuffled labels. Generate 10,000 unrestricted permutations of concept rows with seed 20261112. Keep the MDS coordinates fixed and refit the correspondence after each shuffle. The three models use the same permutation set within each dataset.

The cosine dissimilarity between two input vectors is:

dij = 1 − (xi · xj) / (‖xi‖ ‖xj‖).

The reported effect size is the symmetric Procrustes correlation, r = √(1 − ss), where ss is the residual sum of squares after the normalised fit. Values closer to one indicate better alignment. This is a comparison of the three-dimensional configurations, rather than a direct correlation between entries of the original dissimilarity matrices.

For each test, the upper-tail permutation p-value is (b + 1)/10,001, where b is the number of permuted correlations at least as large as the observed value. Whole concept rows are shuffled; individual dimensions and individual distance entries are not shuffled.

Correspondence with the procedure of Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS)

In Experiment 4a, p. 7, the authors state: “we again reduced embeddings in both spaces to three dimensions via multidimensional scaling, then computed Procrustes correlations between each pair of spaces”.

In the released analysis/get_all_data.r, lines 21–25 compute one minus cosine similarity and lines 30–33 apply cmdscale(..., 3). Lines 86–88 call protest; line 160 gives another example. These are the dissimilarity, reduction and alignment operations used here. For Keha et al. (2026, BRM), CIEDE2000 replaces the colour-profile dissimilarity.

The paper’s SI general Procrustes procedure specifies 10,000 permutations and a SciPy implementation. The released R calls omit the permutation count and therefore use protest’s default of 999. We use the R function with 10,000 permutations. Permuting one configuration generates the same null distribution of relative label correspondences as independently permuting both.

5. Results

All three semantic models aligned significantly with the colour configuration from Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS). Correlations ranged from .3403 to .3891, with permutation p-values of .0001, .0001 and .0003. All three also aligned significantly with the colour configuration from Keha et al. (2026, BRM), with correlations from .1714 to .2135 and p-values from .0029 to .0448.

6. Analysis files

The workbooks contain the input values and extracted word vectors needed to run the analyses. The large fastText model binaries are not required. Keep the files in their supplied directory structure and run the preparation script before the analysis script.

FileContents or purpose
data_raw.xlsxInput concept lists, 70 × 71 mean ratings from Mukherjee et al. (2026, PNAS), 143 full-precision RGB triplets from Keha et al. (2026, BRM), and 300-dimensional vectors for each model. “Raw” refers to the inputs to this re-analysis: the behavioural values are item summaries.
01_prepare_data.RMatches labels, converts the RGB values from Keha et al. (2026, BRM) to Lab, calculates dissimilarities and MDS coordinates, and writes the processed workbook and full item table.
